# Adversarial audit of `RECEIPT_DRAFT.md`

Audit date: 2026-07-18 (America/Los_Angeles)

## Verdict

**REFUTED.** The four implementations do pass the supplied byte-identical
referee, and the three Codex token totals match their retained logs. The draft
as a whole is nevertheless not supported by the supplied evidence: its
pointer word count is false, Haiku prompt purity cannot be checked from the
retained result-only JSON, `arm_luna_tenant/` is not a byte-identical clone of
`base/` with only `scripts/span_ledger.py` added, and the driver-cost and
failed-sandbox claims have no antecedent raw transcript here.

This verdict distinguishes a failed receipt from failed implementations. The
implementations passed; several provenance, prompt, accounting, and
generalization claims did not survive audit.

## 3. Prompt-purity attack

The initial user prompt retained in both `logs/spark.log` and
`logs/terra.log` is exactly:

> Read crate/000.000.INDEX.md and complete the work it authorizes. Repository evidence outranks this prompt. Reply as the index card instructs.

That text has **19 whitespace-delimited words** and 141 characters, not 27
words. It contains no span-ledger task payload. The two visible Codex crate
hand prompts therefore pass the payload-leak check, while the draft's
`27-word` number is directly refuted.

`logs/haiku.json` is a result/usage summary, not a transcript: it contains the
model result, usage, cost, and turn count, but no user prompt. `logs/haiku.err`
is empty. The Haiku hand's prompt therefore cannot be inspected from the raw
evidence supplied. Consequently, `No hand received any task payload in its
prompt` and pointer-only rehydration `3/3` are not established; the retained
evidence establishes prompt purity for **2/3** crate hands. Haiku's passing
script establishes functional success, not what it was told.

`logs/luna.log` does retain the full inline task prompt, matching the tenant
condition described in the draft.

## 5. Cost and call-count attack

- The Haiku cost is supported by its billed meter.
- The three Codex dollar estimates cannot be recomputed from these logs: they
  contain no input/output/cache breakdown and the directory contains no price
  schedule. Their labels as estimates are appropriate, but their amounts are
  not independently verified here.
- There is no retained Fable driver transcript or sentinel-code meter
  supporting `$10.26`. `logs/sol_audit.log` was zero bytes at the start of this
  audit and is the contemporaneous audit transcript, so it is not antecedent
  evidence for the draft.
- The raw evidence contains four completed subject-call records: Haiku,
  Spark, Terra, and Luna. It does not identify “all five subject calls.”
- Even accepting every rounded table amount, the four subject costs sum to
  `$0.127`, and `$10.26 / $0.127 = 80.79`, not 100. “~100x” is loose
  order-of-magnitude rhetoric, not the table's reproduced ratio.

Accordingly, the claims that the experiment's own bill demonstrates a Fable
dominant cost and reproduces “lesson 18” are unsupported by the supplied raw
evidence.

## 6. Sandbox/environment claim attack

Every retained Codex success log declares `sandbox: danger-full-access` at
the start. None records `CreateProcessWithLogonW failed: 2`, a 38.7k-token
Spark failure, a 10.5k-token Terra failure, operator approval, or a
`SOL-ROOT-WRITE-CANARY-V0.4` artifact. Terra's transcript quotes unrelated
workspace-write notes loaded from external memory, but that is not an observed
failure of this arm and does not match the claimed CreateProcess error.

Therefore this directory cannot substantiate the environment finding or its
burn numbers. The missing failure transcripts also prevent an exhaustive
“1 shot” or total-experiment-cost claim: a retained successful session is not
proof that no prior calls occurred.
