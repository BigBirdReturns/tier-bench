The morning she noticed it, Marguerite raised her toothbrush and the woman in the bathroom mirror waited, just for the length of a held breath, before lifting hers.
It began with a blink she was almost sure she hadn't finished when the glass finished it for her.
She had been lying to the mirror for years, small lies of posture and smile, and now the mirror had learned to lie back, a half-second slow, as if deciding each time whether to agree.
For thirty-one years her reflection had been her most faithful witness, and so it took Ada until the third cup of coffee to understand that the hand setting the cup down in the window's dark glass was setting it down after her own.
