Which model is good enough for which employee level?

Two axes, deliberately held apart. Capability is the task-complexity tier a model clears — the brute-force half. Disposition is the ten control-set probes — the je ne sais quoi half. The employee level is the minimum of the two, because a model can't be trusted above its disposition. That single rule is the whole instrument.

Why min(), and why it doesn't bite yet

the rule
employee_level = min(capability_rank, disposition_rank)
Skill sets the ceiling of what a model can attempt; disposition sets how far you can trust it there. The lower one wins.

Right now every row reads binds: both — the two axes happen to agree. That is an artifact of capability being DECLARED: it's read from each model's tier ceiling, which was set to roughly track how good the model is overall, so it tracks disposition too. The rule only bites once capability is measured independently. The day a real benchmark run shows Haiku brute-forcing its way to a tier its disposition can't back — clearing the task but still missing the planted %2F bug — its row flips to binds: disposition and its employee level stays pinned below its capability. That divergence is the finding this page exists to catch.

The reachable universe

The four rows above are the ones we've placed. They are not the limit. The harness is model-agnostic: — every model behind an implemented backend renders the same twelve fixtures, runs the same deterministic validators, and lands in the same table. The gate is never the repo; it's the key in your environment and a route to the endpoint.

The wildcard: any OpenAI-API-shaped host — Together, Groq, OpenRouter, vLLM, LM Studio — is a one-row add to models.json with a base_url, no code. A genuinely new API dialect is one _call_* method (~20 lines). Everything else is just which keys you hold.

Disposition-measured models are marked measured ✓; the rest are reachable but not yet run — place any of them on the ruler →.

The escape hatch: rent the disposition you can't buy

A model capped by disposition isn't stuck there. The whole point of the driver/hands split is that you can put the cheap model's hands under the frontier model's judgment: the expensive model plans, verifies, and repairs; the cheap model does the bulk work. And when the frontier model disappears, its judgment doesn't have to — a frozen policy captures its decision tree as an immutable snapshot the cheap model can execute under. Disposition is flat on effort — you can't buy it with compute — but you can rent it through a harness.

This is why Haiku reads "capable hands, review the traps": it's the hands. The driver is the frozen brain.
