# Asset Floor community and commodity map

This map records candidate communities and standards. Inclusion is discovery, not
qualification. Exact versions, licenses, output rights, execution requirements,
security, and product behavior remain open until a Supplier Foundry adapter closes
them.

## Standards and pipeline communities

| Community | Commodity contribution | Floor seam | First bounded question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alliance for OpenUSD | composition, layers, variants, compliance | `asset.scene.compose/v1` | Which profile preserves named parts, material intent, animation, and variants through an engine round trip? |
| Khronos glTF | delivery, validation, compression, interactivity | format, optimize, behavior | Which extensions survive Unity, Godot, Babylon, and browser import with equivalent behavior anchors? |
| ASWF MaterialX / OpenPBR | portable shading intent | `asset.material.translate/v1` | Which UNDERDRAIN wet metal, rubber, skin, fungus, and water materials translate without visible or numeric drift? |
| ASWF OpenAssetIO | provider-neutral entity resolution and publishing | `asset.resolve/v1` | Can canonical source and engine variants resolve without filesystem paths or one asset manager? |
| C2PA, SPDX, in-toto | provenance, license, component, and process custody | `asset.provenance.attest/v1` | Can one asset retain reference, model, dataset, human-edit, tool, and product lineage across USD, glTF, and engine packages? |

## Communities to enter with concrete artifacts

Do not present the whole AXM architecture. Bring one minimal, reproducible object:

```text
one axm-asset-intent/1
one neutral source
two provider products
one independent verifier
one engine fixture
one exact mismatch
one question
```

Priority conversations:

1. Khronos glTF: behavior-anchor and interactivity portability for valves, doors,
   weak points, and animation triggers.
2. OpenAssetIO: entity traits for canonical source, engine variants, qualification,
   license, credits, and fallback.
3. AOUSD Characters, Motion, and Interactivity: stable part, rig, animation, and
   interaction profiles that do not make a DCC or engine authoritative.
4. CubePart and PartCrafter: user-defined part schemas, completeness, pivots, and
   cross-provider naming.
5. SkinTokens and Anymate: a shared deformation corpus for nonhuman creatures and
   mechanism-aligned characters.
6. 3DCodeBench and Nova3D: code-native fixtures for mechanically editable game props.
7. MaterialX and OpenPBR: wet sewer materials and controlled engine comparison.
8. glTF Validator and engine importer communities: hostile-asset and resource-bound
   conformance fixtures.

## Why the floor can be useful beyond UNDERDRAIN

Most projects optimize one production stage. The floor provides the missing shared
acceptance object across stages:

```text
intent
→ candidate generation
→ semantic structure
→ topology and materials
→ rig and motion
→ physics and LOD
→ engine and gameplay evidence
→ provenance and license
→ accepted product and fallback
```

A community can contribute one provider or verifier without adopting the rest of
the estate. Consumers can adopt the contracts and fixtures without adopting AXM
game law, Tier Bench scheduling, or any current supplier.
